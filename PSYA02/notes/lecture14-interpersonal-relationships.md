## 三、择偶选择性 / Mate Selectivity

人类在选择性伴侣上相当挑剔。  
Humans are quite **selective** in terms of their sexual partners.

就**生理性别（sex）**而言，女性通常比男性更挑剔。  
Females (sex) tend to be more selective than males.

### 3.1 生物学因素 / Biological Factors

为什么有差异？先看生物学因素：  
Why are there differences in mate selectivity? Start with **biological factors**:

**精子数量 vs 卵子数量（Sperm Count versus Egg Count）**：生殖投资成本不同。  
**Sperm count versus egg count**: different reproductive investment costs.

怀孕伴随显著的身体要求与变化。  
Physical requirements/changes that accompany pregnancy.

雄性可以持续繁殖；雌性大约一年只能繁殖一次。  
Males can keep reproducing constantly; females can reproduce only approximately once a year.

### 3.2 社会与文化因素 / Societal & Cultural Factors

就**社会性别（gender）**而言，女性也可能因社会压力而更挑剔。  
Women (gender) may also be more selective than men because of **societal pressure**.

其中包括滥交带来的声誉成本（如 **slut shaming**）。  
This includes reputational costs of promiscuity (e.g., **slut shaming**).

女性被搭讪更频繁，因此可选对象更多。  
Women are approached more often and therefore have more options available to them.

文化**求偶仪式（courtship rituals）**也会影响选择性。  
Cultural **courtship rituals** can affect this selectivity.

### 3.3 Finkel & Eastwick (2009)：快速约会研究

Finkel & Eastwick（2009）用模拟**快速约会（speed dating）**研究求偶仪式。  
Finkel & Eastwick (2009) used a simulated **speed dating** event to study courtship rituals.

关键操纵：谁在旋转换座位（谁主动接近）。  
Key manipulation: whether men or women rotated seats (who did the approaching).

幻灯柱状图大致结果：  
Approximate slide graph results:

| 条件 Condition | 男性说 Yes | 女性说 Yes |
|---|---|---|
| **Men rotated**（传统：男性走动） | 约 **50%** | 约 **43%** |
| **Women rotated**（反转：女性走动） | 约 **43%** | 约 **45%** |

当男性旋转时，男性比女性更常说 “yes”（更不挑剔）。  
When men rotated, men’s “yes” rates were higher than women’s (men less selective).

当女性旋转时，性别差距缩小甚至反转趋势：男性 “yes” 下降，女性 “yes” 上升。  
When women rotated, the gap closed: men’s “yes” dropped and women’s rose.

启示：谁去“主动接近”会影响选择性，不只是固定的性别差异。  
Implication: who approaches affects selectivity—not only fixed sex/gender differences.

## 四、人际吸引 / Interpersonal Attraction

我们基于多种因素选择潜在性伴侣。  
We choose potential sexual partners based on a number of factors.

增加喜欢的因素包括：**外貌吸引力、接近性、相似性**。  
Factors that increase liking: **Physical Attractiveness, Proximity, and Similarity**.

**单纯接触效应（Mere Exposure Effect）**：熟悉带来满足/喜欢（幻灯写 Familiarity breeds content）。  
**Mere Exposure Effect**: familiarity breeds content (familiarity increases liking/comfort).

我们也喜欢那些喜欢我们的人（互惠）。  
We like those who like us.

三种**浪漫吸引（romantic attraction）**：  
Three types of **romantic attraction**:

1. Physical Attraction（身体/外貌吸引）  
2. Situational Attraction（情境吸引）  
3. Psychological Attraction（心理吸引）

---

### 4.1 身体吸引 / Physical Attraction

我们对社交伙伴（性伴侣或其他）最先注意到的之一就是外貌吸引。  
One of the first things we notice about a social partner (sexual or otherwise) is their physical attraction.

起初，这一因素也是最强的。  
This factor is also the most powerful, at first.

**Walster et al. (1966)** 测量大学生对只见过一次的潜在伴侣的看法。  
**Walster and colleagues (1966)** measured university students’ perceptions of potential sexual partners they had met only once.

**外貌吸引是唯一能准确预测是否被对方吸引的属性。**  
**Physical attraction was the only attribute that accurately predicted whether they felt attracted to the other individual.**

**美是普遍有益的，但美的标准并非普遍统一。**  
**Beauty is universally beneficial, but beauty is not universal.**

在所有文化中，被视为美丽都会带来好处。  
Being beautiful carries benefits in all cultures.

但并非所有文化都以相同方式定义美。  
But not all cultures define beauty in the same ways.

例如：某些文化更偏爱超重伴侣，而非“正常体重”伴侣。  
For example: some cultures value overweight partners over normal-weight partners.

---

### 4.2 情境吸引 / Situational Attraction

外貌常是我们最先注意到的，但其他因素也重要。  
Physical attraction is often the first thing we notice, but other factors play a role as well.

或许最大的吸引因素就是**情境吸引**。  
Perhaps the greatest factor in our attraction is simply **situational attraction**.

我们会努力喜欢身边的人，因为必须与他们共处。  
We try hard to like the people we are around, because we have to live with them.

回想**单纯接触效应**：单纯花时间相处就可能更喜欢对方。  
Recall the **mere exposure effect**: simply spending time with someone may make you more attracted to them.

若一起处于**心理唤起（psychologically arousing）**情境，这一效应尤其强。  
This is especially true if we spend time together in **psychologically arousing** situations.

经典例子：**Capilano Bridge 实验**（Dutton & Aron, 1974）。  
Classic example: the **Capilano Bridge** experiment (Dutton & Aron, 1974).

> 核心思路：高唤起（如过吊桥）可能被误标为“对某人的吸引”。  
> Core idea: high arousal (e.g., crossing a suspension bridge) can be misattributed as attraction to someone.

---

### 4.3 心理吸引 / Psychological Attraction

除身体与情境因素外，**心理吸引**也很重要。  
In addition to physical and situational factors, **psychological attraction** is also important.

我们更被与自己**相似**的人吸引。  
We are more attracted to people who are **similar** to ourselves.

相似维度包括：教育水平、宗教、社会经济地位（SES）、人格。  
Dimensions include: level of education, religion, SES, personality.

**Moreland & Zajonc (1982)**：共享活动偏好越多，吸引与感知熟悉度越高。  
**Moreland & Zajonc (1982)**: as shared activity preferences increase, both attraction and perceived familiarity ratings rise.

图示趋势：相似性（共享偏好数 2→8）上升时，Attraction 与 Perceived Familiarity 的平均评分都上升。  
Graph trend: as similarity (shared preferences from 2 to 8) increases, mean ratings of attraction and perceived familiarity both increase.

## 五、社会影响 / Social Influence

人际关系中，我们常试图控制他人。  
In relationships, one aspect of social psychology we all engage in is (the attempt) to control other individuals.

这种控制称为**社会影响（social influence）**。  
We call this control **social influence**.

我们都易受社会影响。  
We are all susceptible to social influence.

本讲重点看两种易感动机：  
We explore two motivations for this susceptibility:

1. **Hedonic Motivation**（享乐动机）  
2. **Approval Motivation**（认可动机）

---

### 5.1 享乐动机 / Hedonic Motivation

回顾 Module 11 的**享乐原则（Hedonic Principle）**：  
Remember the hedonic principle from Module 11:

所有动机基本上都根植于**趋乐避苦**。  
**Hedonic Principle**: all motivation is basically rooted in approach to pleasure and avoidance of pain.

诉诸享乐原则是控制他人最强有力的方式之一。  
Appealing to the hedonic principle is one of the most powerful ways to control others.

#### 例子 / Examples

建立恋爱关系：  
Building romantic relationships:

Relationships → Feelings of Love/Acceptance or Sexual Satisfaction → **Approach to Pleasure**  
关系 → 爱/接纳或性满足 → **趋乐**

超速驾驶：  
Speeding:

Speeding → Caught by Police → Ticket & Fine → Lose money / possible jail → **Pain (we want to avoid this!)**  
超速 → 被抓 → 罚单罚款 → 损失金钱/可能入狱 → **痛苦（我们想回避）**

> 幻灯拼写为 Loose Money，正确应为 Lose Money。  
> Slide typo: “Loose Money” should be “Lose Money.”

#### 奖励也可能适得其反 / Rewards Can Backfire

用奖励诉诸享乐原则可能**反噬（backfire）**。  
But appealing to the hedonic principle with rewards can **backfire**.

**Lepper, Greene, & Nisbett (1973)** 儿童画马克笔实验：  
**Lepper, Greene, & Nisbett (1973)** marker-drawing study with children:

- 一组：画马克笔**有奖**  
  Group 1: given a prize for drawing with markers  
- 一组：可以画但**无奖**  
  Group 2: allowed to draw but given no prize

第二天：无奖儿童仍对马克笔感兴趣；有奖儿童更少继续画。  
Next day: no-prize children remained interested; prize children were less likely to draw with the markers.

为什么？外部奖励可能削弱原本的内在兴趣（过度理由效应相关思路）。  
Why? External rewards can undermine intrinsic interest (related to the overjustification idea).

---

### 5.2 认可动机 / Approval Motivation

另一强大控制机制：诉诸对方对**认可（approval）**的渴望。  
Another powerful mechanism is to appeal to their desire for **approval**.

我们动机上希望别人喜欢并认可我们。  
We are motivated to have others like us and approve of us.

认可动机的一种表现是遵守**规范（norms）**。  
One way approval motivates action is by adherence to **norms**.

例如：约会、电影院、餐厅的行为规范是什么？  
What is the norm for behaviour on a date? In a movie theatre? At a restaurant?

我们“虔诚地”遵守规范，因为这让我们更讨人喜欢。  
We obey these norms religiously because they make us more likeable.

这就是**规范性影响（normative influence）**。  
This is the **normative influence**.

规范性影响常导致我们**从众**（回忆 Asch 从众实验）。  
This normative influence often causes us to **conform** to others’ behaviour (recall Asch’s conformity study).

## 六、攻击 / Aggression

### 6.1 定义与类型 / Definition & Types

**攻击（Aggression）**是以伤害他人为目的的行为。  
**Aggression** is behaviour with the purpose of harming another.

攻击有时也可能“有用/有益”（幻灯：It can sometimes be good）。  
It can sometimes be good (e.g., functionally useful in certain contexts).

#### 两种类型 / Two Types

1. **工具性攻击（Instrumental aggression）**：为达成目标而故意使用暴力（如获取食物）。  
   **Instrumental aggression**: violent behaviour on purpose to achieve a goal (e.g., obtain food).

2. **敌意性攻击（Hostile aggression）**：单纯为了伤害对方（如对威胁的回应）。  
   **Hostile aggression**: violent behaviour solely to inflict harm (e.g., in response to a threat).

---

### 6.2 为什么攻击？挫折—攻击假说 / Why Aggress? Frustration–Aggression Hypothesis

一种解释是**挫折—攻击假说（Frustration–Aggression Hypothesis）**。  
One hypothesis is the **Frustration–Aggression Hypothesis**.

动物在欲望受挫时会攻击。  
Animals aggress when their desires are **frustrated**.

例子：Organism A 挫败了 Organism B 对食物的欲望，于是 B 攻击 A。  
Example: Organism A frustrates Organism B’s desire for food, so Organism B attacks Organism A.

有研究者认为该假说过窄。  
Some researchers argue this hypothesis is too narrow.

人类有时在处于**负性情绪状态**时也会攻击（即使并非特定欲望受挫）。  
Humans sometimes aggress when feeling **negative emotional states** (not only when a specific desire is frustrated).

---

### 6.3 攻击的个体差异 / Individual Differences in Aggression

虽然人人都可能攻击，但有人更容易攻击。  
Although all humans aggress, some are more likely than others to do so.

#### 生物学因素 / Biological Factors

- **遗传学（Genetics）**：解释约 **60%** 的变异  
  Genetics accounts for about **60%** of variability  
- **性别（Sex）**  
- **睾酮水平（Testosterone levels）**

#### 环境因素 / Environmental Factors

- **文化（Culture）**  
- **社会期望（Societal expectations）**

---

### 6.4 生物学与攻击 / Biology and Aggression

个体攻击水平的最大预测因素包括：  
The greatest predictors of an individual’s aggression level include:

- 直系家庭成员中有攻击者（Immediate family members who are aggressive）  
- **性别（Sex）**

男性显著比女性更具攻击性。  
Males are significantly more aggressive than females.

睾酮似乎与此有关。  
Testosterone appears to be implicated.

年轻且睾酮更高的男性更攻击；睾酮更高的女性也更攻击。  
Younger men with more testosterone = more aggressive; women with more testosterone = more aggressive.

#### 睾酮如何起作用？ / How does testosterone work?

睾酮并非在**所有情境**都让人更攻击。  
Testosterone does not appear to make people more aggressive across **all** situations.

它似乎会降低危险情境中的**威胁评估（threat assessment）**。  
It appears to **decrease individuals’ threat assessments** when in danger.

因此，睾酮更高的人可能更不怕报复。  
Thus, people with more testosterone may be **less afraid of retaliation**.

---

### 6.5 环境与攻击 / Environment and Aggression

攻击在进化上具有适应性。  
Aggression is **evolutionarily adaptive**.

那人类是否永远都会攻击？  
So will humans always be aggressive?

过去一个世纪攻击显著下降（更少战争、更少谋杀等）。  
Aggression has decreased significantly in the last century (fewer wars, fewer murders, etc.).

因此攻击**并非不可避免**。  
So aggression is **not inevitable**.

某些文化远比其他文化更暴力或更不暴力。  
Certain cultures are far more (or less) violent than others.

一种证据指向**资源稀缺（scarcity of resources）**。  
Some evidence suggests scarcity of resources.

这又回到**挫折—攻击假说**。  
Recall the **Frustration–Aggression Hypothesis**.

## 七、亲社会行为与利他 / Prosocial Behaviours & Altruism

幻灯以问题形式引入，并给出两类“利他”机制。  
The slides introduce this section with questions and then define two mechanisms of “altruism.”

### 7.1 基本概念 / Core Concepts

**亲社会行为（Prosocial behaviours）**：旨在帮助/有益于他人的行为。  
**Prosocial behaviours**: actions intended to benefit or help others.

**利他（Altruism）**：更强调无私地关心他人福祉（常指不求即时回报的帮助）。  
**Altruism**: helping driven by concern for others’ welfare (often with no immediate personal payoff).

> 注：幻灯提出 “What are prosocial behaviours? / What is altruism?” 但未写满教科书式定义；上面按学习目标“define”做应试补全。  
> Note: slides ask the definitional questions; the wording above matches standard course usage for exams.

非人类动物中的“利他”常见形式：  
What does altruism look like in other non-human animals?

### 7.2 亲缘选择 vs 互惠利他 / Kinship Selection vs Reciprocal Altruism

**亲缘选择（Kinship Selection）**：把“利他”行为延伸到有亲缘关系的个体，从而提高自身基因传递的可能性。  
**Kinship Selection**: extending “altruistic” behaviour to related individuals, thus increasing the likelihood that one’s genetic material will be passed on.

**互惠利他（Reciprocal Altruism）**：提供“利他”行为，并期望日后得到回报。  
**Reciprocal Altruism**: extending “altruistic” behaviour with the expectation that the favour will be returned.

### 7.3 真正的利他？ / True Altruism?

是否存在真正的利他？人类能否真正利他？  
Are there true forms of altruism? Can humans truly be altruistic?

若可以，我们可能是动物界中少有（或唯一）表现出真正利他的物种。  
If so, we may be alone in the animal kingdom in exhibiting (true) altruism.

## 八、合作 / Cooperation

**合作（Cooperation）**是两个或更多个体带来互利的行为。  
**Cooperation** is behaviour by two or more individuals that leads to mutual benefit.

相对于攻击，合作是解决资源稀缺的**另一种方式**。  
Instead of aggression, cooperation is **another way** of solving scarcity of resources.

合作帮助我们获取并保护资源，因此对生存具有**适应性（adaptive）**。  
Cooperation helps us acquire and protect resources, so it is **adaptive** for survival.

那为什么不总是合作？  
Why don’t we just cooperate all the time?

因为合作有风险，且资源是（曾是）稀缺的。  
Because cooperation is risky, and resources are (were) scarce.

---

## 九、群体认知 / Group Cognition

由于合作有风险且资源可能稀缺，我们常选择在**群体**中合作。  
Because cooperation is risky and resources can be scarce, we often choose to cooperate in **groups**.

**群体（Groups）**：一群人有某些共同点，并以此区别于他人。  
**Groups** are collections of people that have something in common that distinguishes them from others.

例子：家庭、团队、宗教、族群、国家。  
Examples: families, teams, religions, ethnicities, nations.

群体也可以自发、任意地形成（如衬衫颜色）。  
Groups can be elicited spontaneously and arbitrarily (e.g., shirt colour).

### 9.1 内群体与外群体 / In-group vs Out-group

**内群体（In-group）**：属于你群体的人。  
**In-group**: individuals who are part of your group.

**外群体（Out-group）**：不属于你群体的人。  
**Out-group**: people who are not part of your group.

我们倾向于对内群体成员持有**积极偏见（positive prejudices）**，并表现出**积极歧视/优待（positive discrimination）**。  
We tend to have **positive prejudices** and exhibit **positive discrimination** toward in-group members.

---

### 9.2 偏见 / Prejudice

**偏见（Prejudice）**是与恨相关的**消极态度（negative attitude）**。  
**Prejudice** is a **negative attitude** associated with hate.

可基于任何共同特征：性别、族群、宗教、原籍国、肤色、性取向、体型等。  
Can be based on any common characteristic: gender, ethnicity, religion, country of origin, skin color, sexual preference, body size, etc.

可超越具体团体，指向任何具有该特征的人。  
Can transcend individual groups and be directed at anyone with that characteristic.

来源：父母、同伴、亲身经历、以及替代性条件作用（vicarious conditioning）。  
Learned from parents, peers, first-hand experience, and vicarious conditioning.

---

### 9.3 歧视 / Discrimination

**歧视（Discrimination）**是针对自己持有负面偏见信念的群体所实施的**不利行为（adverse behaviours）**。  
**Discrimination** is **adverse behaviours** directed at groups toward which one holds negative and prejudicial beliefs.

歧视 = 把偏见付诸行动。  
Discrimination = acting on prejudice.

目的是伤害某一群体或具有特定、被厌恶特征的人。  
Working to harm those from one group or with a specific, disliked characteristic.

### 高频易混对比

1. **Sex vs Gender（择偶选择性）**  
   Sex 侧重生物学（卵子/精子、怀孕成本）；Gender 侧重社会压力与求偶仪式。  
   Sex = biology; Gender = societal pressure & courtship rituals.

2. **Physical vs Situational vs Psychological Attraction**  
   外貌最先最强；情境（共处/唤起）可能最大；心理看相似性。  
   Looks first/strongest; situation often greatest; psychology = similarity.

3. **Instrumental vs Hostile Aggression**  
   工具性为达成目标；敌意性为伤害本身。  
   Instrumental = means to a goal; Hostile = harm is the goal.

4. **Kinship Selection vs Reciprocal Altruism**  
   亲缘：帮亲戚保基因；互惠：帮人求回报。  
   Kinship = genes via relatives; Reciprocal = expected return.

5. **Prejudice vs Discrimination**  
   偏见是态度；歧视是行为。  
   Prejudice = attitude; Discrimination = behaviour.

6. **Aggression vs Cooperation**  
   两者都可应对资源稀缺；合作更互利但有风险。  
   Both address scarcity; cooperation is mutually beneficial but risky.
